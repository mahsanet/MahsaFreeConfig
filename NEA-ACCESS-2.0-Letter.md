Dear NEA/AC team,

I am writing to urge the Bureau's continued support for Holistic Resilience's **ACCESS 2.0** proposal, originally submitted in April 2024 and revised in June 2025 in response to NEA SFOP0010207. The objectives of that proposal remain unchanged — and the two years since its submission have validated them more forcefully than we could have anticipated. What has changed is the scale of the opportunity in front of us, the maturity of the technology we have built, and the size of the community now depending on it. This letter summarizes those developments, the refinements we are making to our approach, and two new strategic angles we believe NEA/AC support should now encompass.

## The objectives remain the same — and the moment has proven them right

ACCESS 2.0 was built around two objectives that we are keeping intact:

1. **Enhance the ACCESS project to develop a secure and shutdown-resilient alternative internet network** that leverages government-independent internet sources — above all Starlink — and innovative technologies, providing VPN access for numerous remote users across Iran via the National Information Network (NIN).
2. **Establish and expand an open community** to support the secure adoption and development of a government-independent, shutdown-resilient internet network.

Since submission, Iran has experienced **two major nationwide internet shutdowns**. Both events confirmed the core premise of ACCESS: when the regime severs the physical infrastructure, software-only circumvention tools — including heavily funded VPN programs — go dark, while satellite-based, government-independent infrastructure keeps people connected. Each shutdown has driven a visible surge in public demand for exactly what ACCESS delivers: **satellite-based, shutdown-proof, independent, and secure internet infrastructure** that ordinary people can own and share. The Iranian public is no longer asking whether such infrastructure is possible; they are asking how to get it. The question before us is whether we can meet that demand while the window is open.

## What we have built since ACCESS 2.0 was submitted

We have not been waiting. The developments since the proposal was considered demonstrate both execution capacity and independent validation of the approach:

- **Starlink adoption in Iran has expanded dramatically.** Government data from late 2024 already estimated roughly 30,000 active Starlink users — a twenty-fold increase over the course of that year — and the true figure is certainly higher, since traffic routed through VPNs and ACCESS companion tools is invisible to the domestic CDN measurements those estimates rely on. The two shutdowns since have only accelerated acquisition. A meaningful satellite terminal base now exists inside the country; the binding constraint is no longer terminals, but safe, simple ways to share and manage them.

- **NasNet Connect has surpassed 5,000 active deployments.** NasNet Connect — the configuration tool that grew directly out of the ACCESS work — enables Starlink terminal owners to securely share their bandwidth with remote users over the NIN. With more than five thousand active deployments, each one serving multiple households and users, this is no longer a pilot: it is a functioning, community-operated alternative network layer inside Iran, precisely the outcome ACCESS 2.0 proposed to build toward.

- **The Open Technology Fund is now supporting the development of NasNet Panel on MikroTik.** OTF has funded the development of NasNet Panel, the always-online successor to NasNet Connect: a containerized management platform that runs directly on affordable MikroTik hardware, turning an ordinary router into an autonomous, censorship-resistant connectivity hub with an Easy Mode installation flow, a fail-closed security posture, and safe self-updating. This is independent validation, by a second U.S.-supported internet freedom funder, of the technical direction ACCESS 2.0 laid out — and it means NEA support would be building on a co-funded, de-risked foundation rather than starting from scratch.

## How we are refining the approach

The objectives stand; our approach to them has matured. With NEA support, we will focus the next phase on making this infrastructure radically more accessible to the public and on hardening the community that sustains it:

**1. Make NasNet truly cross-platform and public-ready.**
NasNet Panel today targets MikroTik RouterOS. To reach the scale the moment demands, we will:

- **Deliver an OpenWrt package with a simple, guided installation**, extending NasNet to the vast ecosystem of inexpensive consumer routers already in Iranian homes and markets. OpenWrt support removes the single largest hardware barrier to adoption and makes bandwidth sharing possible on devices people already own.
- **Develop a Linux-based management dashboard** — work our developer Catto has already begun — so that any Linux machine or mini-PC can serve as a full-featured management hub for shared satellite connectivity. This decouples the platform from any single router vendor and gives operators of larger deployments (buildings, neighborhoods, trusted communities) the visibility and control they need.

Together, these turn NasNet from a tool for the technically committed into infrastructure the general public can install and run.

**2. Strengthen the NasNet community and its support backbone.**
A network of thousands of volunteer operators is only as resilient as the support behind it. We will:

- **Deepen our working collaboration with the ARTICLE 19 helpdesk and the MIAAN Group helpdesk**, formalizing what we have already been doing together: supporting NasNet distributors and operators with Starlink **account resolution** (suspensions, payment failures, verification issues) and secure-use guidance, so that no operator is knocked offline by an administrative problem the regime can exploit.
- **Expand our technical support team** to absorb the post-shutdown surge in demand — growing the knowledge base, multilingual guides, and secure forum channels established under ACCESS, and shortening response times for operators in high-risk situations.
- **Seize the moment.** After two major shutdowns, public interest in independent infrastructure has never been higher. With expanded support capacity and cross-platform tools, we can realistically help **thousands more Iranians deploy NasNet Panel and share their bandwidth**, multiplying every terminal already in the country.

## Two new strategic angles

Beyond the refined core program, developments on the ground and in the satellite industry compel us to add two new angles to the ACCESS portfolio.

**Angle 1: Azadi Ghost — completing the total infrastructure for shutdown-proof access.**
As outlined in our "Pivot to Starlink — Step 2" concept note, the remaining vulnerability in the satellite access model is the last mile. Starlink dishes are expensive, visible, high-value targets that can be triangulated and confiscated; and when the regime shuts down the physical grid, even NIN-based VPN sharing is degraded. **Azadi Ghost** closes this gap: a low-cost (~$25) stealth mesh node, disguised as a benign USB accessory, operating on Sub-1 GHz Wi-Fi HaLow to penetrate dense urban environments up to a kilometer. Deployed alongside NasNet, it allows a single concealed Starlink terminal to securely serve 50+ users through an autonomous, decentralized mesh that bypasses the regime's telecommunications grid entirely — turning a 1,000-terminal base into a 50,000-user network, shielding the high-risk uplink, and remaining fully operational during total infrastructure blackouts. Combined with NasNet (the software and human layer), Azadi Ghost (the physical layer) completes a **total infrastructure** for shutdown-proof access: uplink, distribution, management, and community. We are prepared to execute a fast-tracked hardware engineering sprint to bring this to mass-production readiness within months.

**Angle 2: Direct-to-Cell (D2C) R&D, monitoring, and advocacy for Iran — with a focus on WRC-27.**
Direct-to-cell satellite service is the technology most likely to make independent connectivity in Iran terminal-free — eliminating the smuggling bottleneck entirely by delivering satellite connectivity to ordinary phones. ACCESS 2.0 already anticipated D2C redistribution (Activity 1.8); we now propose a dedicated workstream to get ahead of it:

- **A rigorous D2C feasibility study for Iran**, conducted with spectrum engineers, satellite D2C engineers, and policy researchers, assessing technical coverage, device compatibility, regime jamming and detection capabilities, and safe usage models for Iranian users.
- **Continuous monitoring and advocacy around ITU decisions** affecting Starlink and D2C service to Iran, with a particular focus on **WRC-27**, where spectrum allocations for direct-to-device satellite services will be contested. The Iranian government will be present and organized at these proceedings; the internet freedom community must be as well. We will track proceedings, publish analysis, and coordinate with allied organizations so that regulatory outcomes preserve — rather than foreclose — the possibility of direct satellite access for the Iranian people.

This angle ensures that U.S. investment in today's terminal-based infrastructure connects seamlessly to tomorrow's terminal-free one.

## The ask

We respectfully urge NEA/AC to support ACCESS 2.0 and the program of work described above. The proposal's objectives have been validated by events; the technology has been proven at the scale of thousands of live deployments; a second U.S.-supported funder has independently invested in its core platform; and the Iranian public, after two major shutdowns, is actively seeking what this program delivers. What is needed now is the support to make it accessible to everyone — cross-platform, easy to install, professionally supported, physically resilient through Azadi Ghost, and future-proofed through D2C readiness.

Windows like this one do not stay open. Every month of delay is a month in which demand goes unmet, operators go unsupported, and the regime adapts. We are ready to move immediately, and we would welcome the opportunity to brief your team on the roadmap, budget, and timelines at your earliest convenience.

Thank you for your continued commitment to the free flow of information for the people of Iran.
